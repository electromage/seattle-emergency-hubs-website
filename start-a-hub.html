Start a Hub in Your Neighborhood

You don't need special training, a big budget, or permission. If you care about your neighbors and want your community to be ready when disaster strikes, you have what it takes to start a hub. We'll help you every step of the way.

How It Works

Starting a hub is a series of small, manageable steps. Click each one to learn more.

Send us an email or fill out the interest form below. Tell us a little about yourself and your neighborhood. That's it — no commitment required. We'll set up a conversation to answer your questions and talk about what a hub could look like in your area.

What we'll cover: What hubs do, what's involved, what support we provide, and whether there's already activity near you.

You don't need a crowd — 3 to 5 interested people is enough to get started. Talk to the neighbors you already know: on your block, at the park, at your kids' school. Many people want to be prepared but don't know where to start. You're giving them that starting point.

Tips: Frame it as "neighbors helping neighbors," not doomsday prepping. People respond to connection and community, not fear.

Identify a location where neighbors would naturally gather after a disaster. This is your hub site — the place people in your area will know to go. It should be outdoors or have outdoor space, since buildings may be unsafe after an earthquake.

Good news: We've already identified many potential gathering spots across Seattle — check the Neighborlink Map to see what's near you. You may already have a great location picked out for your area.

Good options: Parks, school yards, community centers, parking lots, church grounds, or even a large front yard. It doesn't have to be permanent — you can change it as your hub grows.

We host regular orientations for new hub leaders — either in person or over video. You'll learn how the hub network operates, what resources are available, and how to run your first neighborhood meeting. We also connect you with experienced hub leaders who can mentor you.

No prior experience needed. We train you on everything: basic emergency response, how to run a drill, communication protocols, and how to keep your hub active and engaged.

Gather your initial group, introduce the concept, and start planning together. We'll provide you with an agenda template, printed materials, and a "hub starter kit" with supplies. Your first meeting doesn't have to be fancy — coffee and conversation at a kitchen table counts.

After that: You're part of the network. You'll join our regular hub leader calls, get access to shared resources, and have a community of experienced volunteers to lean on. Most hubs meet monthly or quarterly — find a rhythm that works for your group.
