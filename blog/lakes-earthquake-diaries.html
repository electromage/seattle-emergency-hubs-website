Lakes act like earthquake diaries

May 28, 2026 · Seattle Emergency Hubs Team

USGS researchers recently highlighted work at Ozette Lake on Washington's Olympic Peninsula, where layers of lake sediment preserve signs of seismic activity going back more than 14,000 years. When strong shaking happens, sediment can move, slump, or settle in recognizable patterns. Over time, those layers become a kind of earthquake diary.

Why does that matter for the rest of us? Because earthquake history in the Pacific Northwest is much longer than written records. The ground has shaken before, and it will shake again. We do not need to predict the exact day to take practical steps now.

Take one small preparedness step. Tonight, before you close your eyes for the night, look up and around. What might fall on you if the ground were to shake during the night? Move it or secure it.
